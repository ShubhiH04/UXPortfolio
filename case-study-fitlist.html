Case study

FitList

A service design concept tackling the self-doubting, trial-and-error experience of buying women's jeans, grounded in behavioral psychology research and prototyped in Lovable using an AI-assisted workflow.

Service Design Journey Mapping User Personas AI Prototyping

Challenge

Women's jeans sizing is fragmented and historically biased, with no universal standard, vanity sizing, and brand-specific modeling that rarely reflects diverse bodies. The result is a self-doubting, trial-and-error shopping experience with a mostly downward emotional trajectory, from optimism at the rack to frustration and giving up entirely.

Approach

I led research and design on a 4-person team, mapping the current customer journey, then grounding the solution in behavioral psychology, applying Kahneman's experiencing-self and remembering-self framework and Hassenzahl's Be-Goals model to design for both moment-to-moment confidence and lasting satisfaction, not just usability.

What I designed

  • A personalized "denim identity" onboarding quiz driving cross-brand fit recommendations
  • A Pinterest-inspired community and friends layer, addressing the isolation of solo shopping
  • Service blueprints and journey maps spanning digital and in-store touchpoints
  • A working prototype built end-to-end in Lovable

Outcome

Summative usability testing validated the community features and denim-identity quiz as the strongest parts of the experience, while surfacing a real limitation, users were unlikely to retake the quiz, which shaped our recommendations for future iteration. FitList reframes jeans shopping from an isolating struggle into a shared, confidence-building experience.
