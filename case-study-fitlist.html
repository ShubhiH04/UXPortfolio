FitList

Buying women's jeans is a guessing game that ends in self-doubt. FitList turns it into a shared, confidence-building experience.

The problem

Sizing has no standard, brands use vanity sizing, and fit models rarely reflect diverse bodies. Our journey map showed shoppers sliding from optimism to giving up.

What I did

Led research and journey mapping, used behavioral psychology to set design goals, and prototyped a denim-identity quiz with a friends-based community. I specifically led the denim identity quiz development and lean canvas research.

The result

Testers responded best to the quiz, the friends page, and the Pinterest-style board. They said they were unlikely to retake the quiz, which is our main open problem.

Additional Thoughts

This project taught me to design from emotion first. Using Kahneman's experiencing and remembering selves, I mapped how shopping should feel at each step, then shaped the screens around it. The friends page, with no public posts or follower counts, came directly from wanting shoppers to feel supported, not judged. The question I'll carry into every project: does the story I designed for the user show up in the product?

From downhill to uphill

The line in each map is the shopper's mood over time. Today it trends down; with FitList it ends high.

Three decisions that shaped it

Design for how shopping is remembered, not only how it feels. Using Kahneman's experiencing and remembering selves, we aimed for control in the moment and an "aha" ending with jeans that fit.

Replace guesswork with a "denim identity" quiz. Inspired by Spotify Wrapped and Buzzfeed Quizzes, it gives personalized, cross-brand recommendations that also work in store.

Make the community personal, not public. Solo shopping is isolating, so we built a Pinterest-style board and a friends page with no public posts or follower counts, just honest feedback from people you know.

The final design

What testing showed

The quiz and denim identity, the Pinterest-style board, the friends page, and shopping the whole look all resonated.

5 User Tests, 20 Minutes Each

What's still unsolved

Users are unlikely to retake the quiz, so recommendations could go stale as fit changes. Future work to address this would involve implementing a feature to update recommendations based on user activity on community pages. Testers also asked for an outfit-of-the-day leaderboard.

Deep dive

Research: storyboard and persona

Our persona, Tiffany, is 29 and loves trying new styles, but a "size 4" in one store fits nothing like a "size 4" in another, and returns waste her time.

Psychology framework
  • Experiencing self: more coherent, controlled effort; a predictable process; progress toward an attainable goal.
  • Remembering self: an "aha" moment, then satisfaction and confidence in the purchase.
  • Be-Goals (Hassenzahl): competence, with pragmatic, hedonic, and eudaimonic goals.
  • Meaning (Huta): restoring coherence so shoppers focus less on frustration and lack of control.
  • Relatedness: moving from an isolated struggle to seeing yourself in others.
Competitive analysis and solution storyboard

True Fit and fitAnalytics live on partner retailer sites and are built for retailers. FitList is a standalone shopper app that works online and in store, with a community.

First prototype

The first versions, made in Figma Make and Claude, had an onboarding quiz, denim identity reveal, recommendations feed, virtual closet, and community page. We then made the community the focus and added the friends page, direct fit sharing, and the whole-look board.
